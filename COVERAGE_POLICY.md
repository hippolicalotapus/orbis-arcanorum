# Inclusive worldwide coverage policy

Orbis Arcanorum documents religious, spiritual, occult, magical, mystical, esoteric, folkloric, paranormal and skeptical traditions worldwide. No tradition is excluded simply because it is unfamiliar, minority-held, controversial, or non-European.

## Examples of traditions and subjects (not exhaustive)

- Haitian Vodou; Louisiana Voodoo; West African Vodun and related distinct traditions
- Lukumí / Regla de Ocha (often called Santería), Ifá, Candomblé, Umbanda, Quimbanda and other African-diasporic traditions, each represented distinctly
- Wicca, traditional witchcraft, contemporary Paganism, Druidry, Heathenry and ceremonial magic
- Indigenous spiritual traditions worldwide, with community-led descriptions and careful handling of restricted knowledge
- Hindu, Buddhist, Jain, Sikh, Taoist, Shinto, Jewish, Christian, Islamic, and other mystical or esoteric currents where relevant
- Alchemy, astrology, divination, grimoires, spiritism, mediumship, folk healing, herbal lore, sacred sites, cryptids, UFO/UAP reports and skeptical investigations

## Editorial commitments

1. Use a tradition's preferred names and spelling; note alternate names without treating distinct practices as interchangeable.
2. Invite practitioner and community perspectives alongside scholarly, archival and critical sources. Attribute each account.
3. Classify evidence and claims transparently. Do not imply scientific proof of supernatural effects or make unsupported health claims.
4. Do not publish private, initiatory, sacred, culturally restricted, or personally identifying material without appropriate authority and consent.
5. Apply consistent marketplace safety, anti-fraud, legality, and prohibited-goods rules to every vendor regardless of belief.
6. The twelve archives are browsing pathways, not a cap on traditions. Support cross-listing and expanding taxonomies as coverage grows.
7. Review for stereotyping, sensationalism and misrepresentation, particularly of living and historically marginalized communities.

Coverage is aspirational and grows through sourced research and community participation; listing a tradition here does not imply its archive entries are already complete.

## Welcoming religious institutions and clergy

The archive is designed to be useful to Catholic priests, Orthodox clergy, Protestant ministers, rabbis, imams, monks, nuns, theologians, religious educators, and other faith leaders as well as occult practitioners and researchers. No visitor is required to affirm another tradition's theology.

Document Catholic and wider Christian subjects including saints and hagiography, relics, Marian apparitions, reported miracles, monastic archives, mysticism, angelology, demonology, historical exorcism practices, pilgrimage, and ecclesiastical history. Clearly distinguish official doctrine, approved devotion, historical documents, popular piety, folklore, private revelation, and disputed claims. Do not imply institutional endorsement or permission to reproduce restricted archives.

Provide source citations, primary-document references, tradition-specific terminology, respectful discussion, and an option to flag inaccurate or offensive descriptions. Marketplace listings and scholarly archive entries must remain clearly separated. Never market harmful ritual services or impersonate clergy or religious institutions.
