# Worldwide Religion and Spiritual Traditions Coverage Standard
Updated: 2026-11-09

## Scope
Document religious, spiritual and ritual traditions worldwide without popularity thresholds. Include living, historical, reconstructed, syncretic, diaspora, minority, indigenous and traditions known only from partial archaeological or textual evidence. Never assume a tradition is extinct simply because its population is small.

## Coverage queues
- Horn of Africa: Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo, Beta Israel, Aksumite religious history, Ethiopian Muslim scholarly traditions, Oromo Waaqeffanna, Eritrean traditions, Ge'ez manuscripts and diaspora contexts.
- Caribbean and African diaspora: Rastafari (plural interpretations), Vodou, Obeah (distinguish diverse historical uses), Kumina, Revivalism, Orisha traditions, Candomble, Umbanda, Santeria/Lukumi, Palo, Ifa and related living traditions.
- Historical and minority West Asian traditions: Manichaeism, Mandaeism, Yazidism, Zoroastrianism, ancient Mesopotamian religions, Hittite, Hurrian and Elamite traditions.
- East, South and Central Asia: Daoist traditions, Shinto, Onmyodo, Chinese folk religions, Bon, Jainism, Sikhism, Adivasi religions, Tibetan and Southeast Asian traditions.
- Indigenous traditions of Africa, Oceania, the Americas and Arctic regions: use community-led sources and do not publish restricted ceremonies, secret teachings or sacred-site locations without permission.
- Europe and Mediterranean: pre-Christian regional religions, Gnostic movements, late antique mysteries, folk Christianity, vernacular magic and reconstructed traditions.

## Editorial rules
1. Distinguish source evidence, tradition-holder interpretation, scholarly debate and unverified claims.
2. Avoid treating religious teachings as validated scientific facts or as fraudulent by default.
3. Record original names/scripts and use accurate English summaries; label translations as verified only when checked.
4. Prioritize primary manuscripts, institutional repositories, peer-reviewed scholarship and community sources. Niche websites are leads, not automatic evidence.
5. Do not host restricted, copyrighted, stolen or culturally sensitive materials without permission.
6. Track provenance, source date, author/collector, translation, community context and item-level rights.
7. Do not use the terms extinct, dead or vanished unless historically justified; note survival and revival where documented.
8. Keep research classifications independent of sponsorship and vendor payments.
